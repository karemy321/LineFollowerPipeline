## v3.1 — steady outputs under real glare

*Added after the first test on the real robot. Sections 1–12 below describe v3 as first
delivered ("v3 before" in the tables here); the numbers in this section are the final ones.*

### The report

On the real robot, with glare from the room light on the path, STEER and POWER did not stay
steady: they kept jumping between several values. This was given top priority over every
simulated case.

### Reproducing it

The first simulator drew glare as a smooth, steady whitening spot — v3 passed it, which is why
the problem was not seen. Real glare is different, and the harness now has it
(`Disturb` type `glareReal`, also `TrackSim.realGlare` for the unit tests):

- the core **clips** to white (flat 255, no colour), with a soft halo;
- the surface (varnish, wood grain, tape) breaks it into **streaks** that are fixed on the floor,
  so they change as the robot moves;
- it **flickers** every frame (mains light against the exposure, auto-exposure hunting);
- the tape is glossier than the floor;
- the spot moves with the camera (a lamp's reflection), so it is present **during calibration** too;
- plus camera **vibration** (rows shift ±2 px), whole-frame exposure flicker (±3 %) and the phone's
  **30 fps** (the closed loop ran at 60 fps before; `sim.fps`, `sim.vib`).

New tools: `Jitter` (scripted drive at 30 fps, each case run with and without glare, frame-to-frame
changes of STEER / POWER compared), `CalProbe` (calibration diagnostics row by row), steering jitter
in `ClosedLoop` (`steerJerk`), `run_suite.sh` / `cmp_suite.py` / `report_v31.py`.

### Causes (found by tracing frame by frame)

1. **The motion estimate drifted.** The path memory is carried from frame to frame by the robot's
   estimated motion. Each frame's correction was added to the speed estimate at 60 %, with nothing
   pulling it back. With glare hiding most of the line, the correction came from 3–5 noisy points:
   the forward speed drifted to 0.18 instead of 0.067 camera heights per frame, the turn rate to
   4°/frame instead of 1.5°, and once to 25°/frame (a curvature prior that was not limited). The
   remembered path bent and slid, the pursuit target jumped toward the robot, STEER jumped
   (5 → 27 → 18 → 5, or ±50 when the bent path left the image).
2. **Every measurement replaced the memory.** Where a glare edge flickers, a path point was measured
   in one frame (at a slightly different place) and not in the next — the path moved back and forth
   with the flicker.
3. **Calibration with the glare in view learned the glare as the line**: a bright band straight
   ahead is what the colour step looked for, and the tape — broken by the glare in several row
   blocks — lost to it (in the jitter set, 21 of 84 calibrations with glare failed or learned the
   glare). The ROI was also cut below the glare: rows that match neither colour ended it, as a table
   edge does.
4. A remembered path that had drifted into the glare was never checked against the line visible
   right next to it.
5. Whole-number outputs flipped between neighbours on a steady path; the "line leaves at the side"
   boost (visibility guard) was switched off from one frame to the next.

### Fixes — all generic (no glare- or tape-specific rule)

| | Fix |
|---|---|
| Motion | A **Kalman filter** on (forward travel, turn) per frame: the prior is the predicted motion with its uncertainty; each measured point adds information in proportion to its precision (measurement + memory variance), outliers are down-weighted (Huber). Many clean points correct it fully; a few noisy ones barely; motion along a straight line (invisible in the line) stays at the prediction. The positions are corrected in full every frame; the motion for the next frame changes at most as fast as a robot accelerates (≈ 2 m/s², 20 rad/s²). The curvature prior is used only while nothing is measured. |
| Memory | Every point carries a **variance**. A measured point is **fused** with its remembered position: the rigid part of the difference (the robot's motion) is applied in full; the rest — the shape of a line that does not change on the floor — is averaged (Kalman gain ≈ 0.4 for a clean point). If a point is far off, or its neighbours are off together in the same direction (the memory was wrong, e.g. a curve seen for the first time), the measurement is taken over at once — no lag on turns. |
| Calibration — line colour | The line is found as a **stripe**: rows are scanned with the floor colour (from the outer 30 % of the image on each side, robustly trimmed, at each row's own light level); every non-floor run with **sharp edges against the floor on both sides** is a candidate; the line is the longest **chain** of runs that continues row by row (about the previous width, where the line leads, of its colour; gaps allowed) — in the image, so it does not depend on the configured tilt. The line colour is the most common run colour in the chain (rows tinted by a disturbance change their tint from row to row and are left out). Glare, shadows and uneven light have soft edges; blobs do not keep one width. If no stripe is found, the cluster-based choice of v3 (now: band in the most of *all* row blocks) is the fallback. |
| Calibration — colours under a disturbance near the robot | The colour samples come from the region near the robot. When a glare is there: the **floor** model is trimmed at 3σ (if ≥ 3 % of the floor cluster are outliers — a glare halo, lighter and greyer floor — they are removed, else the plain cluster model is kept); the **line** colour is taken from the stripe rows with the **highest contrast** against the floor beside them (glare, haze and halos always lower it), and the stripe's own pixels join the line cluster when the samples hold too few line pixels. Without this the black tape fitted both colours (= unreliable) after calibrating in glare, or the line was learned too light. |
| Calibration — ROI | The ROI top is the highest row where the **floor is there** (≥ 20 % floor-coloured samples) and the line was seen with a second line row below; a glare or a shadow over part of the rows in between does not end it, a table edge (no floor beyond) still does. |
| Unconfirmed memory | A remembered path that no measurement confirms for 3 frames gives way to a line found by a fresh search (≥ 5 measured points, running within 45° of the memory). |
| Outputs | Whole numbers with hysteresis 0.8 (steering and power); the visibility guard is released smoothly. (A hysteresis of 4 on the power *target* was tried and removed: it left small lasting offsets.) |
| Steering scale | `steerAutoGain` learns from the steering **actually sent** (including held frames, the guard and short full turns) — before, it saw only the weak commands and lowered the gain of an under-steering robot. |
| Light | The range of plausible floor light levels follows the room light slowly (×0.5 … ×2.5 around the calibration level); a profile with no floor in that range but a floor-coloured majority at one consistent level takes its light level from that majority (the line is always the minority of a profile across it). |

Kept unchanged on purpose (measured worse): letting the light reference itself follow the room
light (broke neutral colour pairs — a black line passed for white floor in deep shadow:
84 → 69 of 100 matrix cases); a faster steering-scale adaptation (no gain); a one-sided seed for
the fresh search (created a wrong lock on a glare edge); a stripe-only calibration without fallback
(failed legitimate synthetic cases).

### Still a problem (open)

- **Gain 0.5 with distractors** beside the line: realistic timing 36/44 (v2 41), camera tilt off by
  10° 30/44 (v2 44). The cause is the motion filter that makes the outputs steady under glare; at
  gain 1.0 (recommended, what the robot uses) all 88 runs complete. Trade-off kept on purpose.
- **Glare that hides almost the whole line** (very large spot, a lamp-tube streak exactly along the
  line): v2 completes more laps (it jitters strongly but keeps a lock); v3.1 follows its memory and
  slows down, then may lose the line. Calibration in such glare fails (or, for black tape on wood,
  can learn the glare) — recalibrate a little further along.
- **Glare right in front of the robot on an S-curve**: 4–20 STEER jumps per drive (clean ≈ 1).
- 3× faster robot with long delay: 141 vs v2 158; POWER steps 0.31 vs v2 0.20 per frame;
  start turned 30° settles about 2 s later.
- The "top to bottom" experiment could not be re-run on the final code (the system stopped it for
  low memory twice, the PC had < 2 GB free); its table (§6.6) is from the earlier v3.
- Nothing is measured on the real robot yet beyond the report that started v3.1.

## 2. What v2 did, and what was kept

v2 detected the line as an inverted "T" (⊥): a 24-row scan strip near the bottom of the image
(steering came mainly from where the line crossed it) plus a row-by-row look-ahead trace up a
column (V-ROI) for tracking and the heading part of the steering. Each frame was converted to a
1-D "line-ness" image (Fisher projection of the learned colours) and thresholded with Otsu.
The problems reported on the real robot follow from that structure:

| Reported problem | Cause in v2 |
|---|---|
| The robot's shadow taken for the line / the lock drifts onto it | The Fisher projection maps shadowed wood toward the line side; Otsu in a strip that contains shadow + tape splits shadow vs lit floor. |
| Look-ahead goes straight up over plain wood in a curve; POWER collapses to 5 | The look-ahead window is a column above the strip; a bright scratch or reflection inside it breaks the run; tracking = lean of that column + its length. |
| Stop-and-go | Tracking jumps whenever the look-ahead length or lean jumps (one row of glare ends the trace). |
| Zig-zag | Steering = strong offset gain at one strip + heading; with 150 ms latency this weaves. |
| **Glare (problem 5)** | Inside the glare spot the tape is whitened; the look-ahead stops at the spot, length → 0 → POWER 12, heading → 0, offset alone → STEER −7. The path above the spot is never looked at. |

**Kept from v2:** colour learning (k-means 2…4 clusters + Fisher discriminant — the Fisher part
now only feeds the legacy colour fields), the exact pinhole floor geometry, the calibration flow
and issue codes, the "line leaves at the edge → ±50 / hidden mid-image → hold" semantics, all
public API. **The whole v2 pipeline is still in the file** and runs unchanged with
`pathTracking = false` — verified bit-exact against `202e70f` (§6.1).

## 3. The v3 approach

Everything is in `LineFollowerPipeline.java` (single file, Java 8, no dependencies, no Android
APIs); the v3 code is the section "v3 PATH TRACKING".

**Colour model — line / floor / unreliable.** From calibration each class keeps its mean colour
(Y, U, V) and covariance. A pixel is compared with each class along a *ray* `g · mean` (a
brightness change of line and floor alike — shadow, uneven light) with `g` limited to the local
light level ×/÷ `lightLocalRange`; the local level is measured from the floor-like samples of the
same profile (median) or, on full-row scans, by a sliding mean. Samples that also match the line
colour are never used to measure the floor's light (with brightness only, a dark line would
otherwise be "floor in deep shadow"). Mahalanobis distances dL, dF: **line** if dL ≤ σ² and the
line fits clearly better (dF − dL > 6, i.e. ≈ 20 : 1), **floor** likewise, otherwise
**unreliable** — glare, a colour patch, an object, a blurred mix, anything that is neither.
A line-ness value (linear between the two colours) places edges with sub-sample precision.

**Calibration.** (1) Colours: a tall region above the robot is sampled; k-means 2…4; floor = the
cluster dominating both edges (or, when a shadow splits it, the largest share of the edges); line
= the cluster forming a narrow band in ≥ half of 6 row blocks (blocks where a disturbance merges
are outvoted); other clusters → a second floor colour, a light change of floor/line, or a
disturbance (excluded). (2) Geometry, `calibFrames` frames: every 8th row from the robot to the
top is scanned (sliding light level), the line is followed upward (prediction + slope); rows with
**clean edges on both sides** give centre and width (a single mixed edge sample counts as a
blurred edge). ROI bottom = just above `bottomIgnoreFrac` (raised past non-floor rows); ROI top =
the highest good row with mostly bad rows above it, minus `roiTopMarginFrac` of the visible
distance. Width model w(v) = A + B·v (robust IRLS) → camera tilt tan T = w(cy) / (f · B) (used if
consistent and within 25° of the configured tilt); line width on the floor; robust line fit of the
centres on the floor → straight-ahead offset and heading zero (|ψ₀| ≤ `steerHeadingCalibMaxDeg`).
The calibrated straight line (plus the part under the robot) is the initial path memory.

**Tracing.** The path is a chain of points `pathStep` apart in the robot frame (x forward, y right,
camera heights). Per frame: (1) the remembered chain is moved by the predicted motion; (2) every
remembered point inside the ROI is measured with a profile across it (`measureAt`: ≤ 72 samples,
~10 per line width, 2×2-averaged pixels, read directly from the Y/U/V or ARGB arrays — no
image-sized buffer is touched) — near → far, with the residual and its slope propagated to the next
point; (3) with `TRACE_BOTH` the gaps between two measured points are re-read with a prediction
interpolated from both sides; (4) the motion correction is fitted (2 DOF: travel along x and
rotation about the axle, weighted least squares on the normal residuals, Huber, weak prior);
(5) every point gets its final position: measured, or remembered + rigid correction + the leftover
residual interpolated from its measured neighbours; (6) the path is extended beyond its far end
(and down to the ROI bottom) into new territory: predict by direction + decaying curvature,
measure, bridge unreadable stretches (≤ `pathMaxGap`), on clean floor try a sharp-corner fan
(±25…100°, never back toward the robot) and then a jump search along the curve; a trace that would
run onto itself is stopped; (7) the chain is re-sampled and a fold-back (> 150° within 3 steps) is
cut. Without memory (start, lost for `trackLostGraceFrames`, or the memory contradicted by clean
floor / by a full line-like run beside it) the view is searched: 8 rows across the full width,
near → far (far → near with `TRACE_TOP_DOWN`), best candidate with the line's width and clean
edges (preferring straight ahead / the side the line was lost to), a direction fan, then extension
both ways.

**Profile analysis** (one place for all measurements): runs of LINE samples (1-sample gaps
bridged). Both edges against floor and width 0.55…1.6× expected → measured (quality ≈ 1). One edge
against floor, the other unknown → the clean edge ± width/2 (0.6) — the "only one border visible +
known width" case. Too wide with an edge where the line's edge is predicted → that edge (0.55) —
the line merged with a blob, or the border of a large dark/bright region only if it is exactly
where the line edge should be. Both edges unknown → only a full-width run (0.35). Weaker evidence
must lie closer to the prediction (half the gate). Nothing acceptable: mostly unknown pixels or a
line-like run that does not fit → **unreliable**; clean floor (≥ 70 %) under the normal light level
→ **absent**; outside the image → out.

**Memory bookkeeping.** Measured → confidence restored, age 0. Remembered → confidence decays with
`pathMemoryFrames` (4× slower while the path is tracked). "Absent" before a measured section is a
gap (unreliable), only trailing absent points end the path. Remembered points on the smooth
continuation of the measured points (quadratic fit, ≤ 0.35 line widths) are confirmed. Path
confidence (for tracking): measured points, or points with measured path on both sides within
`pathMaxGap`, count 1; others their memory confidence.

**Smoothing / control.** Local weighted quadratics x(s), y(s) over ±3 points (tricube ×
confidence × Tukey re-weighting) → positions, tangents; curvature from the change of direction over
±3 points. Steering: pure pursuit from the axle (`pursuitPivotBehind` behind the camera point) to
the first point at `pursuitLookAhead` (shortened to L / (1 + L·κ/2) before sharp bends, ≥ 0.5),
κ = 2·y/d², steering = `steerPerCurvature` × κ × auto-scale, ±50; visibility guard (path leaving
the image side within 1.5 ROI-bottom distances raises the steering toward it, fast attack / slow
release). Tracking: min over the path ahead of the curvature-allowed value relaxed with distance,
the requested turn |κ|, the dead-end length ramp, confidence < 0.5 scaling; light smoothing;
rate limit `powerRiseRate` / `powerFallRate`. Lost logic: measured → normal; remembered path in
unreliable pixels or below the view (blind zone) → follow it (`lineHeld`); clean floor mid-image →
hold `trackHoldFrames` (keeps ramping down if the line's end was seen); otherwise ±50 toward the
side the robot was turning to / the path leads, tracking minimum; after `trackLostGraceFrames`
the whole view is searched.

## 4. Decisions (and why)

| # | Decision | Why (measured where possible) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unreliable is a third class**, not a weak "line" or "floor" | The task's central requirement. A disturbance that fits neither colour model must not become a decision; making it neutral lets memory and continuity decide. Disturbance matrix: v2 mean POWER drop 12.4 → v3 3.1 (§6.2). |
| 2 | Colour match **relative to the local light level** | Shadows, uneven light and dim far rows change line and floor alike; an absolute colour test made a shadowed floor "unreliable" and a dimmed white tape "floor". The local level comes from floor-like samples only (line-like samples excluded — essential for grayscale). |
| 3 | **Floor coordinates** for tracing, fitting and memory ("top to bottom", reading b) | Measured: matrix pass 243 vs 159, closed-loop RMS 1.14 vs 2.82 cm, lost frames 25 vs 216 (§6.6). Only path points are converted; no warp. |
| 4 | **Trace near → far + re-read gaps from both sides** (`TRACE_BOTH`; reading a) | Measured: best matrix (max \|ΔSTEER\| 39 vs 63) and least weaving; far → near alone is slightly worse on disturbances (the far end is thin, dim and next to background), near → far alone lacks the two-sided gap re-read. With a path memory the order matters much less than without one. |
| 5 | **Path memory moved by 2-DOF ego-motion** (travel + rotation about the axle) | A 3-DOF fit mixed sideways shift and yaw when only far points were visible (glare near the robot) and bent the remembered near part by 10°+. A wheeled robot cannot slide sideways; the 2-DOF model fixed it. A turn-rate prior (speed × path curvature at the axle, only on clear curves) carries the memory through blind stretches. |
| 6 | Along-track motion on a straight line is unobservable → memory confirmed by **continuity**, not by age | Remembered points under a glare that moves with the robot never "slide out"; with age-based confidence the power dropped although the path was certain. |
| 7 | **"Absent" only if no measured path follows** + **normal light** | A black line whitened by glare on a white floor looks like bare floor; the line visible beyond it proves a gap. Deep shadow / glare change the light level → never "absent". |
| 8 | **Pure pursuit** with adaptive look-ahead, `steerPerCurvature` = 30 per 1/h | The task asked for it; in steady state on a circle it has zero error when the scale matches the robot. 30 ≈ c = 1 on the simulated robot (wheelbase 0.65 h). Adaptive shortening before sharp bends follows a 4 cm-radius corner instead of cutting it. |
| 9 | **Automatic steering scale** (`steerAutoGain`) | Pure pursuit relies on the robot turning as much as asked; the library cannot know the program gain c or the robot. While following a curve steadily the robot turns as much as the path curves, so path curvature / requested curvature measures it. Helps c = 0.5 (§6.3). |
| 10 | **Visibility guard** (fast attack, slow release) | At low gain (c = 0.5) a tight curve leaves the image side before the robot turns enough; v2 survived those because of its ±50 pin. The guard raises steering only when the path exits the side close in front (never in normal following); a plain on/off version made the robot weave. |
| 11 | **Tracking**: curvature relaxed with distance + requested turn + dead-end length; smoothed and rate-limited | The task asks for smooth power without collapses. A corner 45 cm ahead must not slow the robot to the minimum yet; a robot at an angle must slow even on a straight; rate limits remove stop-and-go. |
| 12 | Memory is replaced by a fresh search **only on evidence against it** | A fresh search whenever "nothing confirmed" switched to a blob outside a curve at c = 0.5 (`ideal` 172 → 149 completed in an intermediate version). Evidence = clean floor where the memory says the line is, or a full line-like run just beside it (the line moved). |
| 13 | ROI found at calibration, **full width**, robot body from `bottomIgnoreFrac` | The task. The top keeps background (floor, chairs beyond a table edge) out; the full width is needed in curves. |
| 14 | Camera tilt **learned from the width per row**, within ±25° of the configured value | The real tilt is uncertain (CHANGES_v2 §9: probably 25–35°, configured 45°). Synthetic flat test images (constant width) would otherwise read as "straight down". `tilt35` regime: §6.3. |
| 15 | Before calibration the v2 pipeline answers | Keeps the documented "processFrame before calibration" behaviour (grayscale + `trackIsBright`). |
| 16 | v2 pipeline untouched behind `pathTracking = false` | Comparison; bit-exact (§6.1). |

## 8. Performance and the camera frame rate

`harness.Perf` (64 pre-rendered 960 × 1280 frames of the oval with distractors, robot weaving,
JIT warmed, 6000 timed frames, this PC, JDK 21, two runs each, same machine state):

| | mean | median | p95 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 | 2.41–2.42 ms | 2.33–2.38 ms | 2.82 ms | 2.94–3.06 ms |
| **v3** | **0.38–0.40 ms** | **0.40–0.41 ms** | 0.54–0.57 ms | 0.79–0.84 ms |

(v2 measured 0.9 ms in October on a less loaded machine; the ratio is what matters: v3 ≈ 6× faster.)
v3 reads only the pixels along the path (≈ 40 profiles × ≤ 72 samples + 3072 grid samples per
frame, 2×2-averaged, straight from the Y/U/V or ARGB arrays) — no projected image, no warp, no
full-frame pass. **Zero heap allocation** per frame in all three formats (`Perf alloc`: 0 / 0 / 0
bytes per 1000 frames; one per-frame array literal found by this check was made a constant).
On a phone expect several times these numbers — far below 16.7 ms (60 fps).

**Does the RoboPhoneApp camera deliver 60 fps at 960 × 1280?** Not verifiable here: no phone was
connected (`adb devices` empty), and the repository has no recorded measurement. From the code:
`MainActivity` asks CameraX for 1280 × 720 with `FALLBACK_RULE_CLOSEST_LOWER_THEN_HIGHER`, but
`ResolutionSelector`'s default aspect-ratio strategy prefers 4:3, which is why the app receives
1280 × 960 (portrait 960 × 1280). It requests a fixed AE range [60, 60] through Camera2Interop on
both the preview and the analysis use case; if the camera does not list [60, 60] in
`CONTROL_AE_AVAILABLE_TARGET_FPS_RANGES`, or 1280 × 960 YUV has a minimum frame duration above
16.7 ms, the request is ignored and the session runs at 30 fps. The app already logs exactly what
is needed: filter Logcat by tag `RoboPhone` — the `CAPS` lines list the AE ranges and the max fps of
every YUV size; the `PROF` lines report the measured delivery rate ("FPS=…") and the processing
time. If 1280 × 960 is not 60-fps capable, set `ANALYSIS_RES` to a 60-fps 4:3 size from the CAPS
list (e.g. 640 × 480) **and** an explicit 4:3 aspect-ratio strategy; v3 adapts to any size at
calibration. The library is not the limit (≈ 0.4 ms).

## 9. Gains in the Blockly block (Ks / Kp)

The Blockly block itself has no gains (it reports Steer and Power); the gains are in the
student's program: `motor steering = (0 − Steer) × c` (Ks = c) and `motor power = round(x × Power/8 + 5)`
(Kp = x/8). **No change is needed.** Recommendation as for v2: **c = 1.0**, power **x = 0** first,
then **x = 1**.

| Regime `real`, all tracks | c = 0.5, x = 0 | c = 0.5, x = 1 | c = 1, x = 0 | c = 1, x = 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| completed v2 → v3 (of 44) | 43 → 42 | 42 → 43 | 44 → 44 | 44 → 44 |
| RMS lateral error cm v2 → v3 | 1.62 → 1.07 | 1.77 → 0.99 | 2.71 → 1.12 | 2.78 → 1.08 |
| sign changes /s v2 → v3 | 0.041 → 0.015 | 0.098 → 0.030 | 0.043 → 0.018 | 0.086 → 0.039 |

`steerPerCurvature = 30` corresponds to c ≈ 1 on the simulated robot (wheelbase 0.65 camera
heights); `steerAutoGain` rescales ×0.5…×2.5 during steady curves, so c between 0.5 and 2 works.
v3 makes c less critical than in v2 (v2 at c = 1 weaved more and ran 2.7 cm off on average).

## 10. Known limitations / still open

1. **Not tested on the real robot.** All numbers are from the simulator (camera height 20 cm,
   tilt 45°, the lecturer's control law, 150 ms latency). The real tilt, height and latency are
   not known; the tilt is now learned at calibration. Verify on the robot with the RoboPhoneApp
   overlay: the path should follow the tape (green), the glare region should be red cells with
   orange (memory) points through it.
2. **Fast robot / long latency (`stress`)**: 135 / 176 completed vs v2 158. Pure pursuit with the
   default look-ahead is tuned for the realistic regime; at 3× speed with 200 ms lag a longer
   `pursuitLookAhead` (1.5–2) or lower power helps. Not tuned further.
3. **Sharp 90° corner (R 4 cm) with distractors at c = 0.5**: 3 failures (right turn); v2 has 3
   on the left turn. After the overshoot, the edge of a large dark region beside the exit leg can be
   taken for the line.
4. **Whole visible path hidden for a long time** (a large glare right in front of the robot while
   the path curves out of the image; strong light gradient while driving): truly unknown — steering
   follows the remembered path and power drops gradually (laps through `uneven`: 4 / 8 vs v2 8 / 8;
   matrix near-curve cases).
5. **Black line, white floor, glare at the far end**: whitened tape = floor; indistinguishable
   from the end of the line until the line is seen beyond it → tracking slows as before a dead end.
6. **Power jerk**: power moves in more small steps (0.34 vs 0.20 per frame mean), while it hits
   the minimum less often; `powerRiseRate` / `powerFallRate` can be lowered for steadier power.
7. **Start turned 30°** settles ~2 s later than v2 (pure pursuit is gentler on a large initial
   heading error).
8. **Junctions / crossings** are not handled specially (a self-crossing track `cross8` is used as a
   stress case only; both versions can fail at a dead end next to another line).
9. **60 fps camera**: not verified (no device) — see §8.
10. The disturbance matrix uses a robot driving exactly on the line (open loop); the closed-loop
    disturbance laps cover the interaction with control.
